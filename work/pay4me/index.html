Radius

I designed payments for students sending tuition, SEVIS and visa fees abroad, often with a family member paying for them.

MY ROLEProduct designer
WHEN2025
FOCUSHigh-stakes cross-border payments, sponsored payments, trust before install
PROBLEM

Students need to pay education and immigration fees abroad on time. A parent or relative may be making the payment.

MY OWNERSHIP

I designed the mobile payment experience and marketing site, including sponsored payments, verification, tracking, currency information and support.

KEY CONSTRAINT

People need to trust the company before downloading the app. The payer and student may also be different people using different currencies.

01The short version

I worked on this product as Pay4Me App. It has since been renamed Radius. It helps international students and immigrants pay tuition, SEVIS, I-20, visa and credential-evaluation fees abroad. I designed the mobile product and marketing site.

02What I owned

My work covered payment journeys, sponsored payments, verification tiers, transaction tracking, currency information and support. I also designed the marketing site. People needed enough information to judge the company before they downloaded the app, so I worked on making the payment language easier to understand.

03The challenge

These payments have deadlines. A delay can affect a student’s admission or visa plans, and the money may come from a family’s savings. The person paying needs to understand the fees, timing and status. They also need to name the student they are paying for. Those needs shaped the payment journey and the information on the website.

04The tricky part

People could research the company on a laptop before paying on a phone. The website needed to answer their questions before they installed the app. Inside the app, sponsored payments needed their own journey because the account holder and student could be different people.

05Discovery to design decisions

Product-design-led, grounded in the category's real deadlines

I looked at what people need to know before trusting the company, and how deadlines affect the payment journey.

The payer and student can be different people. People may also judge the company on the website before downloading the app.

I gave sponsored payments their own payment type and put trust information on the website, with a QR code linking to the mobile app.

I would measure how many first-time sponsors complete a payment. That needs tracking data; I cannot infer it from the designs.

06Decision stories

01 / SPONSORED PAYMENTS

Give sponsored payments their own journey.

Problem
The person funding tuition or immigration fees is often not the student receiving the benefit.
Constraint
A standard personal wallet assumes payer and beneficiary are the same person.
Decision
I designed a dedicated sponsored-payment journey with separate payer and student details.
Evidence
The product structure and payment journeys explicitly model both sponsor and beneficiary.
02 / TRUST BEFORE INSTALL

Put credibility where the decision happens.

Problem
A family may research the company before downloading the app or sending savings.
Constraint
Information inside the app cannot help someone who does not yet trust it enough to install it.
Decision
I put processors, named institutions, coverage, student testimonials and FAQs on the site, followed by a QR code to the app.
Evidence
The published site and project images show this information before the app download.
03 / CURRENCY & VERIFICATION

Show currency and verification before payment.

Problem
Currency and verification limits directly affect what someone can send.
Constraint
People need to know their currency and limits before starting a payment.
Decision
Currency sits with the balance, while verification tier and status sit with identity.
Evidence
The screens show currency with the balance and verification with the account details.
04 / DEADLINES & SUPPORT

Use the names of the fees people need to pay.

Problem
Tuition, SEVIS, visa and credential-evaluation fees have different requirements and deadlines.
Constraint
Broad payment labels can leave people unsure which option to choose or where to get help.
Decision
The product uses the category's own vocabulary and keeps support as a primary destination.
Evidence
The screens show named education and immigration fees, transaction tracking and direct access to support.

07What changed

The design gives sponsored payments a dedicated path. Currency and verification are visible during everyday use. Fee names match the payments students need to make, and the website answers questions before the app download. These are design changes; their individual effect on completion has not been measured here.

08The evidence

The screens show separate payer and student details, visible currency and verification tiers, and direct access to support. The website shows processors, institutions, coverage, testimonials and FAQs. A QR code links to the app. The company-level figures above describe Radius’s traction; they do not isolate the effect of my design work.

09What I’d test next

I would measure first-time sponsored-payment completion and the drop-off from the website to app installation. I would also test whether families sending money abroad for the first time understand the fees and expected timing.
