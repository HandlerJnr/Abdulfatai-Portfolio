01The short version

Horal is a live Nigerian multi-vendor marketplace designed to make buying from independent sellers safer and more transparent. I owned the product design end to end across the mobile app and website, shaping the experience from discovery and product evaluation through checkout, escrow, communication and post-purchase confidence — while also designing the seller side needed to list products, manage stock, fulfil orders and receive payouts.

02What I owned

I designed the customer and seller journeys as one connected marketplace system: browsing and search, product and store presentation, trust signals, checkout, escrow status, in-app communication, seller onboarding and KYC, product listing and inventory variants, order management, store profile and location, seller wallet and earnings, and the responsive website experience around the product.

VALUE

Turned trust from marketing copy into product behaviour: verification, escrow and clear transaction states reduce the uncertainty of buying from a seller a customer has never met.

SCOPE

One end-to-end design system across the public website and mobile marketplace, covering both buyer conversion journeys and seller operational workflows.

RATIONALE

Designed the buyer and seller sides around the transaction lifecycle rather than as separate products, so discovery, payment, fulfilment and payout stay connected.

LIVE PROOF

Horal is live on the web, Google Play and Apple’s App Store. Public listings document escrow, KYC, real-time chat, seller wallet, inventory and order-management capabilities.

03The challenge

A marketplace has a trust problem before it has a checkout problem. The buyer needs enough confidence to pay someone they may not know, while the seller needs tools that make fulfilment, inventory and payout manageable enough to keep the marketplace reliable. The design therefore had to make safety visible without making every screen feel defensive, and give sellers operational depth without exposing that complexity to shoppers.

04The product model

The buyer experience is organised around confidence: find the right product, understand who is selling it, communicate when necessary, pay with protection, then see what happens next. The seller experience follows the same order from the other side: establish identity, create the store, list and manage products, receive an order, fulfil it, and track earnings and payouts. Escrow sits between those two journeys as the system that lets both sides move forward.

05Design decisions

  1. Made verification a visible marketplace signal rather than a hidden compliance step, so shoppers can use it when deciding whether to trust a store.
  2. Treated escrow as a journey with states — payment protected, order moving, delivery confirmed, funds released — rather than a single payment badge.
  3. Designed chat as part of the commerce flow so questions about product details, fulfilment and trust can be resolved without moving the transaction to another app.
  4. Kept seller onboarding, KYC and bank verification connected to the capabilities they unlock, giving compliance a clear purpose instead of making it feel like paperwork.
  5. Designed inventory variants, order management, store profile and wallet as one seller operating system so merchants can run the marketplace side of their business from the same product customers shop in.
  6. Maintained the same trust language and product hierarchy across mobile and web, so the website and app feel like one product rather than separate marketing and transaction layers.

06What shipped

The live product now spans customer shopping and seller operations. Current public app-store notes describe product listing and stock variants, seller wallet and earnings, order management, store profile and location, KYC and store verification, and in-app chat with image sharing. Google Play also documents verified vendors, escrow-protected transactions and Paystack-powered Naira payments.

07Live proof

Horal is available as a live website and on both major mobile stores. That matters for this case study because the work is not presented as a concept: the buyer and seller flows are shipping product surfaces that continue to evolve through live releases.
