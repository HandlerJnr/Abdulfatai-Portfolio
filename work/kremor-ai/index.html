01The short version

Kremor AI generates custom African clothing from prompts and connects the designs to garment production. I designed its storefront, creative workspace and admin platform on contract from June 2024 to July 2026. My work covered flows, screens, AI interactions and the design system. Alongside that, I evaluated 96+ complex AI interaction tasks.

02What I owned

I led product design and design-led research across all three parts. The storefront covered Ankara womenswear, menswear and bags. The workspace supported generating and revising designs. The admin tools covered orders, measurements, artisans, approvals, production, messages, inventory, permissions and audit history. My separate model-evaluation work included forensic benchmarking, dataset annotation and qualitative review of LLM and generative-image output, across 96+ complex interaction tasks.

03The challenge

Ankara, Aso-Oke, Adire and Kente are distinct textiles. The product needed to name them properly and help people explore designs without needing a designer’s vocabulary. It also needed a path from a generated image to a finished garment: measurements, approval, fabric and production all had to be managed.

04The tricky part

A blank prompt field asks people to describe something they may not yet be able to picture. I added starting points while leaving room for their own ideas. Behind the storefront, five staff roles needed to manage production. Actions such as refunds and artisan suspensions needed confirmation, and the team needed a record of who changed an order.

05Rationale: from discovery to decisions

Method: Exploratory, product-design-led, with model-evaluation work alongside

Question explored:How can someone start without knowing design terms? What does the team need to turn the resulting image into clothing?

Design finding:I treated starting prompts and production tools as design requirements. Their usefulness still needs participant testing.

Decision made:I added quick actions and example prompts. I also designed confirmation steps for high-risk staff actions and a read-only audit trail.

Still to validate: Whether the five designed roles match the roles the workshop actually runs on.

This documents requirements and design reasoning. It is not a claim of completed participant usability testing.

06Design decisions

  1. I used the textile names: Ankara, Aso-Oke, Adire, Kente and Afro-fusion. These give people and the generator more specific choices than “African print”.
  2. I added quick actions for design concepts, style options, trends and Ankara pattern variations. The home page also offers example prompts, including “I want an African vibe wear for an event”. People have a starting point even when they do not know the design terms.
  3. I arranged the introduction around personalised designs, AI-powered creativity and unique results. It explains what people can bring, what they can explore and what they can make their own.
  4. I kept the assistant available across the storefront so people can act on an idea while browsing.
  5. The workspace supports revising a design over several attempts. People can add references or voice input and return to earlier work through History. Marketplace stays in the top bar.
  6. Product pages show fabric, care, delivery and how the garment was made. These details let people judge the product’s sustainability claims.
  7. I connected orders, measurements, artisans, approvals, production, messages and exports. That gives the team a documented path from a generated design to a garment made by a tailor.
  8. I mapped permissions for Super Admin, Design Manager, Production Manager, Support Agent and Inventory Manager. Suspending an artisan, cancelling an order, refunding a payment and overriding a status require confirmation. Permissions that cannot be changed are shown as locked.
  9. I designed a read-only audit log with timestamps and the role responsible for each action, including human and system actions. The team can follow what happened to an order.
  10. In the suspension design, the modal first shows the support agent’s twelve open conversations. It offers automatic reassignment by workload and availability, or manual selection with each agent’s workload and a conversation preview visible.
  11. I maintained shared text styles, colour styles and components across the storefront, workspace and admin tools. A Ready for Dev page documented the handoff to engineering.

07Impact and outcome

The design connects exploring an idea, buying clothing and managing production. Starting prompts help people begin. Product pages explain the garment details. Staff tools cover roles, permissions and an audit trail. All three parts share one design system and a documented handoff. Whether the workshop roles match day-to-day practice still needs validation.

08Evidence and limits

The founder’s reference confirms my UI/UX work alongside model evaluation, including forensic benchmarking, dataset annotation and qualitative review of LLM and generative-image output. It is available on the credentials page. I assessed 96+ complex AI interaction tasks, and the build is live. The three parts share a design system and a Ready for Dev handoff. Model evaluation does not establish participant usability results.

09What I would validate next

I would test the starting prompts with people unfamiliar with design terms and the textiles. I would also check whether the five roles and their permissions match how the workshop actually operates before treating that model as settled.
