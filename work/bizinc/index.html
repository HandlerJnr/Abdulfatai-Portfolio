KEY STAGE 01 · STRUCTURE & PROFILE

Two audiences, one platform.

One person manages a business. Another decides whether to choose it. The profile has to serve both.

Problem

Customers need to understand a business and choose a service. Owners need to edit their profile, publish offers and manage the work behind it.

Constraint

The platform was already live. The redesign had to support existing businesses and different service categories without turning owner tools into customer navigation.

Decision

I mapped customer and owner journeys separately, then brought them into one site map. Business Profile 2.0 gives each audience its own view of the same profile.

Evidence

The requirements and designs show what the owner can edit, what the customer sees and how the profile leads to an enquiry or booking.

KEY STAGE 02 · DISCOVERY

Discovery that supports a decision.

A useful card helps someone form a shortlist before opening another page.

Problem

A directory can show plenty of businesses without helping someone decide which one meets their need.

Constraint

Visitors compare businesses by category and location. They also need to know the distance and whether the business is open.

Decision

I brought the service description, location, proximity and open-now status into each card, alongside a direct route to the full profile.

Evidence

The cards show these details before someone opens a profile. I would need tracking data to know whether they help people enquire sooner.

  1. Is it the right business? The name and short service description give visitors a reason to consider the result.
  2. Can I reach it? Location and distance make proximity visible before opening the profile.
  3. Is it open? The opening-hours signal supports a time-sensitive choice.
  4. What happens next? “View Profile” leads into the richer identity, offers and service information.

KEY STAGE 03 · MAP & AI PROPOSAL

From search to booking.

The assistant’s value depends on what a visitor can actually do after the conversation.

Problem

A visitor may know the service, place and time they need without knowing which local business to search for.

Constraint

Recommendations need to match the service, location and actual availability. A conversation alone cannot confirm those details.

Decision

I proposed linking Bizzy to nearby businesses and the map. It would clarify the request, show matching businesses and help the visitor move toward booking.

Evidence

The Figma proposal shows the conversation moving from a service request to a time, a shortlist and a confirmation. It demonstrates the intended interaction, not a verified production integration.

Need & location→Relevant businesses→Availability & booking
Relevance and ROI are decision gates.

I would compare assistant-led discovery with direct search, looking at relevant enquiries and completed bookings. ROI is undisclosed. The reported retention increase does not establish the effect of this proposal.

LEARNINGS & NEXT STEPS

A shared profile needs explicit boundaries.

What I learned

Writing owner and customer requirements side by side helped me see what each view needed. Mapping the journeys separately first made it easier to decide what belonged on the shared profile.

What I would test next

  • Do location, distance and open-now signals shorten the path to a relevant enquiry?
  • Which owner tools encourage meaningful return visits, rather than profile edits alone?
  • Does assistant-led discovery improve completed bookings enough to justify its cost?
