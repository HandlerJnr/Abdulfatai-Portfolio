01The short version

Until April 2024, I worked on corporate and retail digital banking, including onboarding, identity checks, accounts, transfers and approvals. My role also covered campaign design and front-end work. The product shipped across Gambia, Guinea, Sierra Leone and Burkina Faso. ITSS recognised my Vista contribution with its Most Outstanding Intern of the Year award.

03The challenge

A finance manager might manage accounts for several companies across different countries. Each company has its own accounts, currency and payment rules. Some transfers need another person’s approval. The screens needed to show which company someone was acting for and what they could do next, without presenting every detail at once.

04The tricky part

The same design had to work across four subsidiaries with different languages, currencies and regulations. I put English and French selection at sign-in so people could understand the app from the start. Corporate journeys also needed their own account and approval patterns because one person could act for several companies.

05Rationale: from discovery to decisions

Method: Requirements-led discovery with product and engineering teams

Question explored:Could the retail account patterns support someone managing several legal entities, currencies and approval mandates?

Design finding:Corporate users needed a visible company context and a clear place to find payments waiting for their approval.

Decision made:I put the entity beneath the user’s name and showed approval counts near the top of the accounts screen.

Still to validate: Post-launch adoption and task-completion behaviour, which I did not have access to.

This documents requirements and design reasoning. It is not a claim of completed participant usability testing.

06Decision stories

01 / ENTITY CONTEXT

Make the legal entity impossible to miss.

Problem
A finance manager may hold mandates over several companies and markets.
Constraint
Acting in the wrong entity can mean moving the wrong company's money.
Decision
I put the legal entity directly beneath the user’s name so they can check it before acting.
Evidence
The shipped navigation and account views carry entity context before a transaction starts.
02 / APPROVALS

Treat approvals as work, not notifications.

Problem
Corporate payments often wait on another person's mandate before money can move.
Constraint
A notification badge understates a blocking task with financial consequences.
Decision
Pending bulk, single and other approvals moved above the fold with their own counts.
Evidence
The corporate account experience surfaces approvals as a primary destination.
03 / ACCOUNT SCALE

Design for dozens of accounts, not a tidy demo balance.

Problem
Corporate users can manage many accounts across entities and currencies.
Constraint
A flat account list becomes unusable at real portfolio scale.
Decision
I added default and custom views, sorting and account-type filters. Available balance comes first.
Evidence
The account system supports saved views and local-currency context across the portfolio.
04 / ONE SYSTEM, FOUR MARKETS

Keep a shared design, with room for local requirements.

Problem
Gambia, Sierra Leone, Guinea and Burkina Faso needed one coherent banking product.
Constraint
English and French, currencies, regulatory wording and local requirements differ across the subsidiaries.
Decision
I built localisation into the shared design system and put language selection at sign-in.
Evidence
The system shipped across four subsidiaries. I reviewed implementation against the shared designs.

07Impact and outcome

The product shipped across Gambia, Guinea, Sierra Leone and Burkina Faso with one shared design system. It supports multiple entities and currencies, visible approval tasks and account views for larger portfolios. ITSS recognised my contribution with its Most Outstanding Intern of the Year award. I did not have access to post-launch adoption or task-completion data.

08Evidence and limits

The ITSS award letter specifically names my UI/UX contribution to Vista Bank. The full letter is on the credentials page. The product shipped across four subsidiaries, with entity, currency and language visible in the experience. This supports the delivery and recognition claims; it does not establish a measured improvement in customer behaviour.

09What I would validate next

I would test whether corporate customers can identify the active entity before making a transfer. I would also check whether they understand the approval counts as tasks waiting for them. These questions need testing with real mandate holders.
