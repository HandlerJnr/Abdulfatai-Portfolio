One shared UI language, adapted for four Vista subsidiaries.

Vista needed a recognisable banking experience across The Gambia, Guinea, Sierra Leone and Burkina Faso. The UI had to work in English and French while accommodating each subsidiary’s currency, regulatory copy and banking requirements.

UI/UX Designer & Front-End Developer · ITSS · May 2022–April 2024

I helped design corporate and retail banking journeys across the four subsidiaries and built a shared design system that allowed local differences. I designed Vista’s debit card and the screens for its loan application flow. My work also covered onboarding, account views, transfers and approvals within the Temenos Infinity implementation, alongside campaign graphics and HTML/CSS implementation review.

The decisions behind the interface

  • Keep the design language consistent across subsidiaries. I used shared components and interaction patterns so each local experience remained recognisably Vista, while allowing language, currency and regulatory copy to vary.
  • Make language available at sign-in. English and French selection sits at the start of the journey, rather than being left inside settings after a customer has already had to log in.
  • Keep local and company context visible. Currency stays with account balances, and the active legal entity appears beneath the user’s name so corporate users can see which company they are acting for.
  • Adapt the shared system to corporate banking. Pending approvals have their own counts and prominent placement. Account sorting, filters and custom views support people managing multiple accounts.

What was delivered

The banking design shipped across four localised subsidiaries using one shared system. The work brought a consistent Vista interface to English- and French-speaking markets while preserving the local information customers need.

ITSS’s Most Outstanding Intern of the Year award names my Vista Bank UI/UX work. The four-subsidiary rollout is delivery scope; post-launch usage and task-completion results were not available to me.

Contribution & evidence

At ITSS, I helped adapt one Vista UI design language for four subsidiaries as part of the Temenos Infinity implementation. I designed Vista’s debit card and the screens for its loan application flow. Shared components and interaction patterns provided consistency; language, currency and local requirements shaped each subsidiary’s experience.

Designing cross-border payments for the student and the person paying.

Education and immigration payments have deadlines. A parent or relative may be paying for a student in another country, using another currency.

Product designer · mobile payment experience and marketing site · 2025

I designed sponsored payments, verification, currency visibility, transaction tracking and support. On the website, I organised the information families need before trusting the service enough to install the app.

A personal note

This project changed how I think about payment UX. Sometimes the person making the payment isn’t the person whose future depends on it.

The decisions behind the interface

  • Give the sponsor a clear payment route. I treated the payer and student as different people in the journey, rather than forcing sponsored payments into a personal transfer.
  • Put payment constraints before commitment. Currency appears with the balance, and verification status appears with identity, so both are visible before a payment starts.
  • Build trust before the download. The site uses named institutions, coverage, student testimonials and FAQs, followed by a QR-code handoff to the app.
  • Use the language of the task. Tuition, SEVIS, WES and visa payments are named directly, with tracking and support easy to reach.

What was delivered

The design connects the website’s trust-building role to the mobile payment journey. Sponsored payments, verification and transaction status are treated as core parts of that experience.

Radius, formerly Pay4Me, reports more than 120,000 registered users, more than 1,500 five-star reviews and average delivery of six hours on business days. These are company-level figures, not results attributed to my design work. Read the published figures.

Giving guests and property investors different routes into the same business.

A guest is choosing a place to stay. An investor is deciding what a property share means and what it costs. Combining those decisions would give each audience the wrong information.

Product designer & researcher · 2024

I designed responsive guest-booking and fractional-investment journeys, supported by research into how guests shortlist properties. The two experiences share a visual system while keeping their decisions separate.

The decisions behind the interface

  • Separate the entry points. Guests begin with stays; investors begin with property investment. Each route introduces the information needed for that decision.

Connecting the steps after a patient finds a doctor.

A patient’s journey continues through coverage, prescriptions, finding medication and follow-up. Those steps involve different organisations and can leave the patient without a clear next action.

End-to-end product designer · 2026

I designed the connected care journey across patient, provider, HMO and pharmacy experiences. The work covers onboarding, care discovery, consultation, coverage, prescriptions and continued care.

The decisions behind the interface

  • Keep access possible without insurance details. A Skip for Now route lets the patient continue instead of treating missing coverage information as a dead end.
