The first month in the UK is an ordering problem

A research concept exploring which arrival tasks international students need to complete first, and why. The dependency model remains a hypothesis.

Jamiu Abdulfatai

UXUI | Product Designer

[Contents]

01Who touches it next

A year ago I left a studio job to start Field Notes, a one-person design and code practice in Helsinki. I had two clients, a long list of side projects and a vague plan to keep things small. Twelve months later the plan is less vague, and some of it survived contact with reality.

The first month in the UK is an ordering problem

Settle is my research-led concept for helping international students understand which arrival tasks unlock others. Rather than publish another list of documents and appointments, I mapped dependencies such as enrolment, proof of address and banking. The idea came from preparing for my own move to Brighton for my MSc. The task order is grounded in published guidance, but it remains a hypothesis until students with different arrival experiences confirm or challenge it.

I framed the question and research plan

  • Everything on this project is mine: framing the problem, designing the study, writing the survey and interview guide, running the competitive review, and the design that follows. I started my MSc at the University of Brighton in September 2026. This project grew from questions I faced preparing to move to the UK. My own experience is one perspective, not evidence of how every student arrives; that is why the research plan includes different arrival circumstances.
  • It is not a checklist problem. It is a dependency problem. The clearest case is the proof-of-address loop: a high-street bank asks for proof of a UK address; the usual proof is a university bank letter, which the university will only issue after enrolment and after a term-time address is on record; and some landlords want a UK bank account before they will sign a tenancy. Each counter is individually doing nothing wrong. A student who does not know the order can lose a week moving between the three.
  • The information is not missing — it is published, accurate and free. That rules out the obvious product, which would be another list. It also means the design has to earn trust against GOV.UK without impersonating it, and has to be useful to someone whose situation may not match the sequence I experienced. The hard part is establishing whether hidden ordering really is the cause of the pain, or whether the pain sits somewhere else entirely — appointment availability, language, or simply not knowing who to ask.
  • Research status · October 2026: survey questions, an interview plan and source review are documented here, but participant findings are not yet published. The dependency model remains a hypothesis, not a validated result.

Model the tasks as a dependency graph rather than a list — the unit of design is the edge between two tasks, not the task itself.

What exists today is the study design and the problem model: a dependency map, a seven-stage journey map with pain ratings, three falsifiable hypotheses with their measurement criteria, and a scope boundary. No outcomes are claimed because none have been measured yet. This page is deliberately published in that state — it shows how I work before I have answers, which is the part of the process a finished case study usually hides. It will be updated as findings land.

The study design and problem model are documented: a dependency map, a seven-stage journey map, three falsifiable hypotheses and a scope boundary. Participant findings are not yet published, and no measured outcomes are claimed.

Useful guidance still leaves students stuck

The honest risk is that I have modelled this problem from documentation rather than from anyone's experience, including my own. A dependency graph drawn from official guidance describes how the system is supposed to work, not how it fails. The study is built to attack that: the hypotheses are written to be falsifiable, the interviews reconstruct each participant's actual sequence rather than checking mine, and the survey asks what was hardest before it asks about ordering at all.
